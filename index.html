About and credits

Research and education prototype. Not a medical device and not for clinical use: do not use it to diagnose, plan or treat. The anatomy outlines start as automatic drafts and are shown to students only after a second reviewer has accepted them; even then they are teaching aids, not measurements.

Data and software

Main scan. One case from: Blagosklonova E, Dolotova D, Polunina N, Grigorieva E, Pakhomov D, Krylov V, Gavrilov A. Open dataset of annotated DICOM images of head CTA for intracranial aneurysm detection. Zenodo, 2025. doi:10.5281/zenodo.15697196. Licence: CC BY 4.0. Data paper: doi:10.3390/data11040074. The publisher anonymised the images and removed the face. Changes made here: the case was cropped, resampled to 0.6 mm and anatomy outlines were added. The original authors do not endorse this tool.

Outlines. Drafted with TotalSegmentator (Apache-2.0): Wasserthal J, et al. TotalSegmentator: robust segmentation of 104 anatomic structures in CT images. Radiology: Artificial Intelligence 2023;5(5):e230024; built on nnU-Net: Isensee F, et al. Nature Methods 2021;18:203-211. Some outlines come from simple rules in this project. Every outline shown to students was then checked by a person.

Software. The 3D view uses three.js (MIT licence).

How to use

  1. Three slice views, one 3D model. Axial looks up from the feet, coronal from the front, sagittal from the side. The coloured lines show where the other two slices cut.
  2. Scroll over a slice view to move through the scan. Click or drag to move the crosshair; all views follow.
  3. Zoom with the + and − buttons, a pinch, or Ctrl/Cmd + scroll. Right-drag (or Alt-drag) moves a zoomed slice; double-click or "Fit" resets it.
  4. Click a structure on a slice or in 3D to select it. It lights up everywhere and the card on the left explains it.
  5. Click a name in the list to jump straight to that structure. Click a group title to open or close it; the eye beside a name shows or hides that one structure, and the group's checkbox does the whole group. Show only this on the card isolates a structure; Show all puts things back.
  6. CT window changes the brightness range: "Bone" for bone detail, "Soft tissue" for glands and muscles, "Brain" for the brain.
  7. Yellow dots are named points for openings and canals that have no outline. A dot shows on a slice when the slice passes through it.
  8. Quiz tests you both ways: finding a named structure on the slices, and naming a highlighted one.

On screen, the patient's right is on the left of the axial and coronal views, as in radiology. Slices are 2.5 mm apart in the original scan, so coronal and sagittal views look softer than axial. "Sharpen edges" makes borders crisper but also exaggerates noise and can draw faint halos that are not anatomy.

Author mode (?author=1) also shows unreviewed drafts, the Landmarks tab for placing points and the Review tab (author pre-check, then radiologist sign-off). Students use the plain address and see only structures whose outline the reviewer marked Correct.
